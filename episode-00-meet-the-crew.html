Cal Mercer

Welcome to The GLBFF Huddle. I’m Cal Mercer, alongside former GLB quarterback Peyton Marsh-Tit and our producer, Marcos Vega. This is a new show, which means Peyton has a brand-new place to make predictions he’ll later insist were taken out of context.

Peyton Marsh-Tit

We’re eight seconds in and you’re already rewriting history.

Marcos Vega

Peyton Marsh-Tit

Marcos Vega

Cal Mercer

And that, more or less, is the crew. The show is new. The arguments are not.

Peyton Marsh-Tit

That’s important context. Cal and I have been disagreeing about football for years, and Marcos has apparently spent that entire time building an evidence locker.

Marcos Vega

Archive.

Peyton Marsh-Tit

Evidence locker.

Marcos Vega

Archive with excellent metadata.

Cal Mercer

Which is somehow worse.

Cal Mercer

Here’s what we’re actually doing. The GLBFF is full of teams fighting through League schedules, division races, playoff pushes, championships, Ladder games, and careers that can stretch across multiple seasons. We want to follow the stories that come out of all of that—not just repeat the numbers back to you.

Peyton Marsh-Tit

Right. If a quarterback throws for five hundred yards, I want to know what that looked like. Was he carving people up? Was the protection ridiculous? Did the receivers take over the game? Or did somebody hand him the ball before kickoff and say, “Good luck, we've abandoned the concept of running?”

Cal Mercer

And then I want to know whether five hundred yards actually meant the offense was good.

Peyton Marsh-Tit

See, that sentence right there is why people hide spreadsheets when you enter a room.

Cal Mercer

Numbers have never hurt anybody.

Marcos Vega

You made a ranking system for party dips.

Cal Mercer

That was Useful!

Peyton Marsh-Tit

You had a 67 point scoring system that made Barb cry.

Cal Mercer

There were CLEAR performance differences.

Marcos Vega

You used viscosity as a metric.

Peyton Marsh-Tit

Thank you! This is the man who’s going to tell me football can’t be about feel.

Cal Mercer

Football can absolutely be about feel. I would just occasionally like the feeling to survive contact with evidence.

Peyton Marsh-Tit

And that’s basically us. Cal brings the numbers. I bring the perspective of somebody who has actually stood behind center and had a pass rusher arrive much earlier than the brochure suggested.

Cal Mercer

All while Marcos keeps the entire operation running.

Marcos Vega

And occasionally explains to both of you that the thing you are confidently arguing about did not happen.

Peyton Marsh-Tit

That feels like analysis.

Marcos Vega

Cal Mercer

Marcos knows the game, too. He’s just smart enough not to volunteer for every argument.

Marcos Vega

I have buttons. There are easier solutions.

Peyton Marsh-Tit

I don’t like how much joy you got from saying that.

Marcos Vega

You shouldn’t.

Cal Mercer

We’ll talk about the games that matter, players having ridiculous seasons, teams changing as they move through the Federation, and League races as they develop. We’ll use Ladder results when they actually tell us something useful, and we’ll go back into career history when knowing the history makes the current story better.

Peyton Marsh-Tit

And we’re going to appreciate the weird stuff. Weird names. Weird scores. Weird stat lines. A defense ruining somebody’s afternoon. A running back deciding that being tackled is more of a suggestion. Football gives you enough strange material on its own. You don’t have to manufacture it.

Cal Mercer

That last sentence was suspiciously responsible.

Thank you.

Marcos Vega
